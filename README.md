# HAITIAN GANG WARS

(First called "Rot Riot" - some file names still say so.)

You are **Jean-Baptiste, "JanJan"**, a Haitian soldier. The gangs bought a
sorcerer's powder and now send armies of the dead. Take the country back,
one gang's ground at a time: every map has its own gang, and its leader is
the boss. The gangs and their leaders are invented.

A top-down twin-stick zombie arena shooter. First playable draft, made in
Godot 4 with GDScript. All art is placeholder shapes and all sounds are
generated by code - nothing is taken from any other game.

## Play it in Godot

1. Download **Godot 4** (standard version, not ".NET") from
   <https://godotengine.org/download> - version 4.7 or newer is best.
   It is a single program, no installer needed.
2. Start Godot. In the Project Manager click **Import**, choose the
   `project.godot` file in this folder, then **Import & Edit**.
3. Press **F5** (or the Play button at the top right). The game starts on the
   main menu.

If Godot says the project was made with a different version, accept - it
works on 4.5 and later.

## How it plays

- **PLAY** opens the map screen. There are five maps and each has **100
  waves**. Clear wave 100 and the next map opens - with much tougher zombies
  and bigger rewards.
- **The map is big** - far bigger than the screen - and it starts hidden
  under **fog**. The fog clears wherever you walk and stays clear for the
  rest of the run. The **minimap** in the bottom right corner shows the whole
  map: where you have been, where you are (the white arrow), zombies (red
  dots), weapon crates, dropped gear and the boss.
- **Treasure chests:** four are hidden somewhere on the map every run. When
  the fog over one clears it appears on the minimap as a gold mark. Walk
  into it to open it: you get a piece of gear (yours to keep, even if you
  die) and a handful of coins. Chests opened in later waves hold better
  gear. The counter above the minimap shows how many you have opened.
- Zombies claw out of the ground around you in waves that keep getting
  bigger, wherever on the map you are - you cannot outrun a wave, and a boss
  you leave behind digs its way back to you. Every 5th wave brings a boss.
  **Every boss you beat is a checkpoint**: next time you can start that map
  from the wave after it (choose it on the map screen), with a weapon crate
  waiting beside you.
- **Play with friends (up to 3 players, online).** On the main menu choose
  **PLAY WITH FRIENDS**. It takes three small steps, best done while you
  are chatting or on a call:
  1. One of you presses **HOST A GAME**. A code appears in big yellow
     letters (it looks like K7QM-X2PD-9AB4). Send it to the others
     (**COPY CODE** copies it).
  2. The others type or paste it into the box on the same screen and press
     **JOIN**. Sometimes that is all. More often, after a few seconds, the
     joining player's screen shows a **REPLY CODE**.
  3. The joining player sends that reply code back to the host. The host
     types it into the box and presses **LET IN**. A few seconds later the
     player is in.
  Then the host chooses the map and starts, and everybody's game begins
  together. (Why the reply code? Home internet routers let nobody in who
  was not spoken to first. With the two codes, both games speak to each
  other at the same moment, and both routers let it through. Nobody has to
  change anything in a router.)
  - **All gold is shared:** every coin anybody picks up counts for all of
    you, and each of you keeps the whole amount. Gear is shared the same way.
  - **Zombies are tougher** and there are more of them, the more players
    there are.
  - Everybody plays their own character, with their own level, skills, gear
    and pet. The others have a coloured ring and their name.
  - If you run out of health you fall instead of dying. Another player can
    **pick you up by standing beside you** for 3 seconds. The run is lost
    when everybody is down.
  - Everybody needs the **same version** of the game. Mac and Windows
    players can play together.
  - **If somebody cannot get in:** let another player be the host and try
    again - some internet connections (mobile hotspots, some student or
    office networks) cannot be reached from outside, but can reach others.
    Join between runs: the host cannot type a reply code in the middle of a
    fight.
  - Two players in the **same house** use the same-house code the host's
    screen shows (it looks like 192.168.0.12) instead of the yellow one.
  - To work out the codes, the game asks a public "what is my internet
    address?" server (run by Google and Cloudflare - the same kind every
    video call uses). Nothing about you or your game is sent to them.
- **Perks.** Every 5 waves, right after the boss, the game stops and shows
  you **three perks: choose one** (click it, or use the arrow keys and
  Enter, or a gamepad). More health, more damage, faster reloads, bullets
  that go through zombies... A perk stays with you **until that run ends**;
  taking the same one again makes it stronger. The small symbols above
  your weapon show what you have, and the pause menu spells it out. If you
  start a map from a later wave you first choose the perks you would have
  had by then. With friends, everybody chooses their own, and the next wave
  waits until everybody has.
- **Know your zombies.** Besides the plain Shambler, the fast Runner and the
  big Brute there are four with a trick of their own. Each one **warns you
  first**: it stops, swells up and changes colour for a moment. The first
  time you meet one, the game tells you what it does.
  - **Gobber** (yellow, puffed cheeks) - spits at you from a distance. The
    glob is slow: sidestep it, or hide behind a rock. Shoot the Gobber while
    it swells up and it has to start again.
  - **Chucker** (big, grey, carries rocks) - lobs a lump of rubble at the
    spot where you stand. A **red ring** on the ground shows where it will
    land: get out of the ring.
  - **Gasbag** (round and bloated) - bursts when it dies, or when it reaches
    you. The blast kills the zombies around it too, so pop it while it is in
    a crowd - and never right next to you.
  - **Lunger** (thin, blue-grey, claws) - crouches, then leaps at you in a
    straight line. Step aside and shoot it while it lies dazed.
  Zombies that attack from a distance only do so when you can see them.
- You play **JanJan**. He starts with a pistol. **Crates** drop near you
  with a better weapon that lasts about 20 seconds: SMG, shotgun, machine
  gun, rifle, sledgehammer, launcher, flamethrower or chainsaw. The crate
  shows a big picture of the weapon, and its name underneath in the colour
  of its rarity (white = common, green = uncommon, blue = rare). An arrow at
  the edge of the screen points to crates out of view.
- **Careful where you shoot:** crates are wood. Your bullets, flames and
  explosions damage them, and a crate that is shot to pieces takes its
  weapon with it.
- **Reloading:** guns reload by themselves when empty, or press R to reload
  early. The shotgun loads one shell at a time and you can fire in between.
- **Dual wielding:** pick up a crate of the weapon you are already holding
  (it shows "x2") and you get one in each hand. Works with the pistol, SMG,
  shotgun and machine gun. A pistol crate always gives you two pistols.
- Kills give **XP**. The blue bar along the bottom is your character level;
  each level makes you a little stronger and gives a **skill point** (the
  highest level is 100, and it is meant to take months to reach).
- Spend skill points on the **Skills** screen. It is a plain list: one row
  for each thing you can improve - HEALTH, DEFENSE, EVASION, DAMAGE, ATTACK
  SPEED, CRITICAL, MOVE SPEED, LUCK and PET BOND - with a **+1** button at
  the end of the row. A rank costs 1 point at first and a little more as the
  stat gets high; there is no limit. When a stat reaches **rank 15** you
  also get its special power (the row tells you which). You can buy extra
  skill points with coins, or take all your points back for a fee.
- Every weapon also has its **own level**, earned by killing with it. In the
  **Armory** each weapon has one plain page: a row per upgrade (power,
  shooting speed, stability, reload speed, how often its crate appears, how
  long it lasts) with a "+1" button, and three **perks** that open at weapon
  levels 5, 12 and 20 and change how the weapon works (bigger magazine,
  bullets that go through more zombies, a wider swing, a bigger blast...).
- **Gear.** Now and then a zombie drops something to wear - you will see a
  pillar of light in the colour of its quality, and an arrow at the edge of
  the screen points to it. Walk over it and it is yours for good, even if you
  die. Rings and amulets only drop from bosses.
- The **Character** screen shows JanJan in the middle with his nine slots on
  either side (helmet, chest, gloves, pants, boots; amulet, two rings, belt),
  his stats underneath and your **inventory** on the right. Click a piece in
  the inventory to wear it; click a slot to take it off. JanJan changes with
  what he wears - on this screen and in the game.
- **Forging.** FORGE in the inventory opens the forge. Two identical pieces
  of the same quality can be forged into one of the next quality: common
  (white), sturdy (green), fine (blue), superior (purple), legendary
  (orange). Sturdy always works, fine works 90% of the time, superior 40%,
  legendary 10%. If it fails you lose one of the two pieces - and for
  superior and legendary there is a chance of losing both. The game asks you
  twice before a forging that can fail.
- **Pets.** At character level 18 a stray dog, Scraps, starts following you
  and fights at your side. There are five pets, from common to legendary
  (**Pets** on the main menu shows them all and what each one needs: a
  level or a cleared map, then coins). One comes along at a time. A pet bites
  zombies that come close to you, and it **adds to your own damage** - a rarer
  pet adds more, up to 15% for the legendary one.
  How much of that you get, how hard the pet bites and which tricks it knows
  (fetching coins and gear, mauling, guarding you, a frenzy) all come from
  **PET BOND**, a row on the Skills screen (it opens at level 18).
- Zombies drop **coins**. Everything you earn is saved.

## Getting the newest version (Mac)

Close Godot, then double-click **Update Rot Riot.command** inside the project
folder. A Terminal window opens, downloads the latest version and replaces the
folder. Your coins, level and upgrades are stored elsewhere and are kept.
Then open the project in Godot again. The version number is shown in the
bottom right corner of the main menu (now v0.12.0).

After an update that changes the upgrade screens, points you had already
spent are handed back so you can place them again in the new trees.

## Tuning the game

Open `scripts/config.gd` in Godot (double-click it in the FileSystem panel).
Every number that matters is there with a comment: player speed, weapon
damage and fire rates, zombie health, wave sizes, how much harder each map
is, XP and skill point costs, skill prices and special powers, weapon upgrades
and perks, every piece of gear with its drop chance and forging odds, every pet
and what it adds, and all the "feel" values - screen shake, hit stop, knockback, recoil.
Change a number, press F5, try it.

## Replacing placeholder art and sounds

- Sprite: put a PNG named like the sprite in `assets/sprites/`
  (for example `shambler_0.png`, `player.png`, `boss_0.png`), drawn facing
  right at twice the size listed in `scripts/autoload/art.gd`. (JanJan's own
  pictures, `player.png` and `hero_front.png`, are normally drawn from the
  gear he wears; a PNG replaces that with one fixed picture.)
- Sound: put a `.wav` or `.ogg` named like the sound in `assets/sfx/`
  (for example `shotgun.wav`). The names are listed at the top of
  `scripts/autoload/sfx.gd`.

## Exporting builds for friends

One-time setup: in Godot choose **Editor > Manage Export Templates >
Download and Install** (about 1 GB).

Then **Project > Export...** - two presets are already set up:

- **Windows Desktop** - select it, click **Export Project...**, untick
  "Export With Debug", save as `RotRiot.exe`. You get one file. Zip it and
  send it. Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box the first
  time: click **More info > Run anyway**.
- **macOS** - select it, click **Export Project...**, untick "Export With
  Debug", save as `RotRiot.zip`. Send the zip. Your friend unzips it and gets
  `Rot Riot.app`. Because the app is not signed with a paid Apple developer
  account, macOS blocks it the first time: open it once, then go to
  **System Settings > Privacy & Security**, scroll down and click
  **Open Anyway**. (On older macOS: right-click the app > Open.)
  This works from a Windows PC too - you do not need a Mac to export.

Before changing the macOS preset for a real release, set your own
"Bundle Identifier" in the export dialog (it is `com.rotriot.game` now).

## Automated checks (optional)

From a terminal in this folder, with `godot` on your PATH:

```bash
godot --headless -d --fixed-fps 60 -- --autotest
```

```bash
godot --headless -d --fixed-fps 60 -- --inputtest
```

```bash
godot --headless --fixed-fps 60 -- --bench=250
```

The first plays through the whole game automatically (menus, maps, the
skills, weapon upgrades, gear and forging, pets, every weapon, boss, death, levels, save file), the
second checks every control, the third measures speed with 250 zombies. See `CLAUDE.md` for the design notes.
